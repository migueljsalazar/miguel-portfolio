# Research synthesis

Captured: August 28, 2026

## Research question

How can Miguel's portfolio feel like a distinct creative place, with the
explorability of DORF and the atmosphere of Deaton Chris Anthony's studio,
without copying either one?

## Evidence and limits

The research used public page extraction for text, links, and image lists, plus
a logged-out rendered-browser review for DORF and Instagram. Third-party
screenshots and source images were used only for analysis and are intentionally
excluded from version control.

Instagram findings are limited to the public, logged-out profile surface that
was visible on the capture date. They are not an exhaustive review of the
account.

## The useful tension

DORF is a polished digital diorama. Studio 75 AV is an evolving physical
system. The original opportunity is the tension between them:

- **precise shell, active interior** — a calm spatial composition containing
  visibly unfinished work;
- **slow atmosphere, fast access** — an optional reveal without making a busy
  visitor wait;
- **technical routing, human evidence** — wires, nodes, drafts, annotations,
  collaborators, and proof of outcomes;
- **nostalgia as a tool, not a costume** — older interfaces or objects should
  explain Miguel's process, not serve as generic retro decoration.

## Recommended creative direction: The Working Room

The home page is an original room assembled from Miguel's real practice. It is
not a replica of a famous studio. Each object has a narrative job:

- a monitor or projection surface opens the selected-work index;
- a proof sheet, notebook, or pinboard opens process notes;
- a personal object opens the About story;
- a phone, intercom, or door opens Contact;
- visible cables or routes connect projects to shared capabilities.

The room should feel useful before it feels luxurious. A visitor should sense
that work happened here five minutes ago.

## Original visual language

These are concept tokens inferred from the source atmosphere, not sampled
brand colors.

### Color

- `paper`: `#E8E5DD` — warm off-white walls and editorial ground
- `ink`: `#151515` — near-black text, leather, and equipment
- `birch`: `#C8A66F` — pale working wood
- `amber`: `#E99A35` — task light and active focus
- `window-blue`: `#6487A5` — cool spatial depth
- `signal-yellow`: `#E4D43B` — cables, focus rings, and status
- `route-red`: `#A94A3F` — rare routing or alert accent

Use amber and blue as light, not large brand fills. Signal colors should have a
functional meaning and remain accessible against their backgrounds.

### Type

- A neutral sans serif for primary reading and case studies.
- A disciplined monospace for labels, coordinates, dates, and system status.
- Avoid a wall-to-wall terminal aesthetic; it would collapse the direction
  into retro pastiche.

An open-source starting pair is Instrument Sans plus IBM Plex Mono. Final type
should be tested with Miguel's actual name, project titles, and body copy.

### Material and image behavior

- off-white acoustic texture, dark leather, exposed brick, pale wood, brushed
  metal, screen glow, and imperfect cables;
- wide environmental frames mixed with close technical details;
- a light film-grain treatment only on authored photography, never on text;
- mostly square corners, with irregularity reserved for personal objects;
- generous negative space around dense clusters of work.

### Motion and sound

- Camera motion should be short, deliberate, and interruptible.
- Objects can wake through light, focus, or a small mechanical response.
- Sound is off by default. Any sound layer requires an explicit control and
  must never block understanding.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` receives a static room and direct transitions.

## Experience rules

1. Show Miguel's name, positioning, and a direct **View work** action before
   the immersive scene finishes loading.
2. Offer **Enter the room** as progressive enhancement, not a gate.
3. Every hotspot has a visible label, keyboard focus, and equivalent semantic
   link.
4. Project pages are normal, indexable pages rather than content trapped in a
   canvas or third-party iframe.
5. Mobile receives a designed 2D composition, not a tiny desktop room.
6. Use only original, licensed, or explicitly approved assets.

## Source-derived decisions versus assumptions

Observed facts are recorded in the source table. The Working Room concept,
palette, type pairing, object map, and product architecture are original
recommendations. They remain provisional until Miguel supplies the actual
portfolio content and personal visual references.
